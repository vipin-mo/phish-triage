# Rubric for rating the analyst's summaries

I rate 50 analyst outputs by hand (25 phishing, 25 legitimate emails) from `data/analyst/rating_sheet.csv`, which is
generated by `python -m phish_triage.evaluation.analyst_eval run` and stays local because it quotes dataset emails.
Each output gets 1–3 on four criteria; the averages are reported, and I say in the README that I rated them myself.

| Criterion | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **correct** | The verdict and summary match what the email really is | Right overall, but misses or overstates a point | Wrong verdict, or the summary misdescribes the email |
| **grounded** | Every claim is backed by a quote or a signal I can check | One claim isn't backed by anything shown | The summary relies on things not in the email or the signals |
| **clear** | A non-expert would understand it in one read | Understandable but wordy or uses jargon | Confusing or misleading |
| **useful** | Tells the user exactly what to do (or that nothing is needed) | The action is vague | No action, or a harmful one (e.g. "click to verify") |

Rules I follow while rating:

- I read the email before the summary, so the summary doesn't shape what I think the email is.
- I rate what the user would see: the final verdict, the summary, the recommended action and the indicators that
  survived grounding. A score is kept when the sheet is rebuilt only if all of these are unchanged.
- A legitimate email raised one step (to "suspicious" or "needs human review") is correct = 2; called phishing, 1.
- Useful is 3 when the action and the summary agree. "No action" plus "check the sender if unsure" in the summary
  is two different messages, so 2.
- A safe-looking email the analyst correctly calls safe can still score 3 on every criterion.
