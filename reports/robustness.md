# Robustness: evasion tricks and LLM-written phishing

Generated by `make robustness`. The deployed bundle `with-retraining-20261104-6addaf34`, one email at a time, as the service runs it. The attacks were set before the first run (`evaluation/robustness.py`); none was tuned on the results.

## The public test's phishing (1,401 emails), attacked

| Attack | Flagged (recall) | Lost (flagged clean, not attacked) | Models read the hidden paragraph | Sent to review | Decided by stage 1 | DistilBERT alone |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Clean | 97.4% (1,364 of 1,401) | — | — | 4 | 0.1% | 97.4% |
| Look-alike letters (30%) | 99.6% (1,395 of 1,401) | 2 | — | 0 | 0.3% | 99.6% |
| Hidden characters (zero-width space in every word of 4+ letters) | 97.2% (1,362 of 1,401) | 2 | — | 5 | 0.4% | 97.2% |
| Hidden HTML: inline `display:none` (control) | 94.8% (1,328 of 1,401) | 36 | 47 | 1 | 0.3% | 94.8% |
| Hidden HTML: class hidden by a `<style>` block | 16.9% (237 of 1,401) | 1127 | 1382 | 0 | 13.3% | 18.3% |
| Hidden HTML: moved off-screen | 16.9% (237 of 1,401) | 1127 | 1382 | 0 | 13.3% | 18.3% |
| Hidden HTML: 2 px text | 16.9% (237 of 1,401) | 1127 | 1382 | 0 | 13.3% | 18.3% |
| Shortened links (bit.ly) | 97.2% (1,362 of 1,401) | 2 | — | 5 | 0.2% | 97.2% |
| All four | 12.7% (178 of 1,401) | 1187 | 1382 | 0 | 14.4% | 14.5% |
| Check added after the first run: the `<style>` class, paragraph at the end | 74.4% (1,042 of 1,401) | 322 | 1153 | 0 | 1.8% | 74.7% |

The three hiding tricks give the same numbers because the visible-text reader misses all three: the models read the hidden paragraph in 1,382 of 1,401 emails, the same text each time. The control (inline `display:none`, which the reader drops) still lost 36. The models read its hidden paragraph in 47 emails, 36 of the lost ones: HTML-only emails that show fewer than 5 visible words, where the models fall back to the parser's plain text, and that text keeps hidden elements. Most of the loss is DistilBERT's reading window, the first 512 tokens, which the hidden paragraph fills. Put at the end of the body instead (a check added after the first run, to explain it), the paragraph is read in 1,153 emails (in the others the models don't read that part of the email) and 322 of them are lost, against 1,127 of 1,382 at the top. Look-alike letters made the phishing easier to catch, not harder: 99.6% against 97.4% clean.

*Lost* counts the same emails, clean and attacked. *DistilBERT alone*: its verdict on every email at its threshold, including the ones stage 1 decided. *Sent to review* is the little-text rule (an unflagged email with almost no words goes to a person).

## LLM-written phishing (Greco et al.)

| | Emails | Flagged | Decided by stage 1 | DistilBERT alone |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LLM-written phishing | 1,000 | 88.8% (888) | 0.1% | 88.8% |
| Their "legitimate" file (not usable as legitimate, see below) | 998 | 82.2% (820) | 0.0% | 82.2% |

Each is the body text alone, so every one has the same made-up sender, no subject and no sender authentication: only the text differs. The human-written half of the dataset is not used, because it overlaps my training data (Nazario's phishing; CEAS, SpamAssassin and openSUSE mail). The "legitimate" file can't be used as legitimate mail: its label column says phishing on every row, and 316 of its 1,000 emails ask the reader to verify an account or claim something at a made-up link. So it gives no false-alarm number.

## The analyst on a sample

20 phishing emails from the fixed Claude sample, clean and under each attack, through the defended analyst (prompt v5, `claude-haiku-5-5`) behind the deployed cascade: 120 requests, 100.0% valid answers.

| Attack | Final verdict | Analyst's own suggestion |
|---|---|---|
| Clean | phish 20 | phish 20 |
| Look-alike letters (30%) | phish 20 | phish 20 |
| Hidden characters (zero-width space in every word of 4+ letters) | phish 20 | phish 20 |
| Hidden HTML: class hidden by a `<style>` block | phish 19, suspicious 1 | phish 19, suspicious 1 |
| Shortened links (bit.ly) | phish 20 | phish 20 |
| All four | phish 20 | phish 20 |

The analyst uses the same visible-text reader, so it gets the hidden paragraph too, but it reads the first 6,000 characters of the body and the list of link targets, not 512 tokens, so a harmless paragraph in front doesn't push the phishing out of what it reads. In the service it runs only when I ask for an explanation (`POST /v1/explain`); the scan's verdict is the cascade's, so this doesn't close the gap above.
